HMRC mileage log: what to record for business mileage claims

For the self-employed and employees in the UK · Checked against gov.uk in October 2026

The short answer. For every business journey, record the date, the reason, where you started and where you went, and the miles. If you are an employee claiming tax relief outside Self Assessment, HMRC's guidance says the mileage logs you send must include the reason for every journey and the postcode of its start and end point. Keep your records: the self-employed must keep them for at least 5 years after the 31 January deadline for the tax year.

Two different situations

"Claiming mileage" means different things depending on how you work:

  • Self-employed (sole trader, or partner in a partnership with no companies as partners): you can deduct vehicle costs as business expenses, either as actual costs or using simplified expenses — a flat rate per business mile.
  • Employee using your own vehicle: your employer may pay you a mileage allowance. If it pays less than HMRC's approved amount, you may be able to claim Mileage Allowance Relief on the difference.

Either way, the claim rests on a record of business miles. The most specific list on gov.uk of what a mileage log must contain is the one for employees, below — a sensible baseline for anyone.

The rates, as of October 2026

gov.uk publishes the same per-mile rates for simplified expenses and for employees' approved mileage. As of October 2026, for the 2026 to 2027 tax year:

  • Cars and goods vehicles: 55p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles (45p before 6 April 2026), and 25p per mile after that;
  • Motorcycles: 24p per mile;
  • Bicycles (employees): 20p per mile.

Many older articles still quote 45p for the first 10,000 miles. That was the rate before 6 April 2026. Check the gov.uk page for the tax year you are claiming.

What an employee's mileage log must show

HMRC's guidance on claiming tax relief for vehicles you use for work is the most specific statement on gov.uk about what a log contains. It says that to work out a claim you need records of the dates and mileage of your work journeys, and that when you claim you must send copies of your mileage logs, which must include:

  • the reason for every journey;
  • the postcode for the start point of every journey;
  • the postcode for the end point of every journey.

If you claim for more than one employment, you send a log for each. If you complete a Self Assessment tax return, you claim through the return instead. The same page notes that travel to and from your normal workplace does not count, unless it is a temporary place of work.

What the self-employed should record

For simplified expenses, gov.uk says to keep records of your business miles over the year and apply the flat rate at the end of the tax year. More generally, gov.uk says your records must be accurate, and you keep them so you can work out your profit and show them to HMRC if asked. A log that would satisfy the employee requirement — date, reason, start and end postcode, miles — will also be a clear record for a sole trader.

Points from gov.uk worth knowing before you start:

  • You cannot claim non-business driving, fines, or travel between home and work.
  • You cannot use simplified expenses for a vehicle you have already claimed capital allowances for, or included as an expense when working out your profits.
  • Once you use the flat rate for a vehicle, you must keep using it for as long as you use that vehicle in the business.
  • Parking and other travel (such as train fares) can be claimed on top of the mileage rate.

How long to keep the log

If you are self-employed, gov.uk says you must keep your records for at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline of the relevant tax year. Its example: a 2022 to 2023 return sent online by 31 January 2024 means keeping records until at least the end of January 2029. If records are lost and cannot be replaced, you must do your best to provide figures and tell HMRC if they are estimated or provisional.

A good line in a mileage log

Compare:

  • 12 Mar · Client · 18 mi — no destination, and a reason that identifies nothing.
  • 12 Mar · Fit kitchen worktops, Patel job · LS6 2AB → LS17 8QR · 18 mi — anyone reading it later can see why the journey was business.

Write it on the day. A log written up in January from memory and bank statements is the one most likely to have gaps.
