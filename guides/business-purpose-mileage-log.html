How to write the business purpose in a mileage log

Examples for the UK, Australia and Canada · Sources checked in October 2026

The short answer. Write down who or what the trip was for, specifically enough that a stranger could see why it was business: the client, job, site, supplier or task. "Visit Mrs Okafor to measure for blinds, job 214" works. "Client" or "work" does not. Write it on the day.

Every authority asks for it

Distance and dates are only half a mileage record. The other half is why you made the trip, and the three tax authorities below all name it explicitly:

  • UK — HMRC. For employees claiming tax relief on vehicle use, gov.uk says the mileage logs you send must include the reason for every journey, along with the start and end postcode.
  • Australia — ATO. A logbook must record, for each journey, the reason for the journey — "such as a description of the business reason or whether it was for private use".
  • Canada — CRA. For each business trip, the CRA's list is date, destination, purpose and kilometres driven.

The purpose is also the one field nothing can fill in for you. A map or a GPS trace can show where a car went; it cannot show that the trip was to quote for a job rather than to visit a friend who lives on the same street.

What makes a purpose specific

A useful purpose usually answers two questions: who (client, customer, supplier, employer, site) and what for (quote, delivery, inspection, meeting about a particular thing). A reference you can match to other records — an invoice number, a job number, a property address — makes it stronger.

Vague → specific

  • Client meeting → Quarterly review with Harbour Dental, accounts for Q3
  • Work → Install bathroom fan, job 1182, 14 Rowan St
  • Supplies → Collect timber order #5521 from the yard, Fyshwick
  • Site visit → Inspect roof leak for strata claim, Unit 4, 22 Elm Rd
  • Showing → Show 9 Ashgate Rd to the Okonjos (listing 33)
  • Bank → Deposit business takings, Headingley branch
  • Errand → Post signed contract to Pell & Co, Kirkstall post office

Several stops in one trip

Record the stops together if your authority allows it. The ATO, for example, says two or more journeys in a row on the same day can be recorded as a single journey. A purpose like Deliveries: Lee (inv 301), Novak (inv 302), Ruiz (inv 305) keeps the reason for each leg.

Regular runs

If you make the same trip every week, the purpose can be the same every week — as long as it is still true and specific. Weekly stock check, Unit 7 storage is fine fifty times. Work fifty times is not.

Trips that usually do not count

Writing a good purpose does not turn a private trip into a business one. For example, gov.uk says the self-employed cannot claim travel between home and work, and employees cannot claim travel to and from their normal workplace unless it is a temporary place of work. If a trip was mixed — business and personal — say so honestly, and ask an accountant how to treat it.

Write it on the day

A purpose written the same day is detailed because you still remember it. One written at the end of the year is usually a guess dressed up as a record. The ATO's 2024 instructions asked for logbook entries at the end of the journey or as soon as possible afterwards, and all three authorities expect records that are accurate. A two-minute habit each evening is far easier than reconstructing a year.

A quick checklist for each line

  • Date of the trip.
  • Purpose: who, and what for.
  • Where you started and where you went (UK employees: the postcodes).
  • Distance — from odometer readings if you take them (the ATO's logbook asks for start and end readings).
  • Written today, not later.
