CRA vehicle logbook: what to record, and how the simplified logbook works

For self-employed people in Canada · Checked against canada.ca in October 2026

The short answer. For each business trip the CRA asks you to log the date, destination, purpose and kilometres driven, and to record each vehicle's odometer reading at the start and end of the fiscal period. After one full year of logbook records (a base year), you may be able to use a three-month sample logbook in later years, as long as the result stays within 10% of the base year.

Why the logbook matters

If you use a vehicle both for business and personally, you can only deduct the business part of its costs. The CRA's page on motor vehicle records says the best evidence of business use is an accurate logbook of business travel kept for the entire year, and that you can deduct motor vehicle expenses only when they are reasonable and you have receipts to support them. To get the full benefit of a claim, keep a record of the total kilometres you drive and the kilometres you drive to earn business income.

The full logbook: what to record

For each business trip, the CRA lists:

  • date;
  • destination;
  • purpose;
  • number of kilometres you drive.

And for each vehicle:

  • the odometer reading at the start and end of the fiscal period;
  • if you change vehicles during the fiscal period, the dates of the changes and the odometer reading when you buy, sell or trade the vehicle.

If you use more than one vehicle for your business, keep a separate record for each, showing the total and business kilometres and the cost to run and maintain it. Each vehicle's expenses are calculated separately.

The simplified logbook

Keeping a full logbook every year is the gold standard, but the CRA offers a shortcut. You keep a full logbook for one complete year to establish a base year. After that, you can keep a three-month sample logbook and use it to work out business use for the whole year, as long as usage is within 10% of the base year's results. You must be able to show that the base year is still representative of how you normally use the vehicle.

The CRA's formula is:

(Sample year period % ÷ Base year period %) × Base year annual % = Calculated annual business use

The CRA's own example: a base year with 49% annual business use, of which April to June was 46%. In a later year, a sample logbook for April to June shows 51%. The calculation is (51% ÷ 46%) × 49% = 54%, which the CRA says it would accept in the absence of contradictory evidence, because it is within 10% of the base year — not lower than 39% or higher than 59%.

If the calculated figure moves by more than 10%, the base year no longer represents that year. The sample logbook then only counts for the three months it covers, and the rest of the year has to be based on an actual record of travel or other records. In that case, the CRA suggests considering a new base year by keeping a logbook for a new 12-month period.

Practical tip: if you plan to rely on the simplified method later, make sure your base-year log can show business use by period, not just a single annual total, so the sample months can be compared like for like.

How long to keep it

According to the same CRA page, records and supporting documents generally have to be kept for six years from the end of the tax year they relate to. The full 12-month base-year logbook must be kept for six years from the end of the last tax year it is used to establish business use — which can be much longer than six years from when you wrote it.

What makes a logbook entry useful

  • A purpose that identifies the trip. "Meeting" on every line does not show why a journey earned business income. Name the client, job or supplier.
  • A destination, not just a distance. The CRA lists destination as its own item.
  • Odometer readings at both ends of the fiscal period. They give the total kilometres that your business kilometres are measured against.
  • Written at the time. Entries written the same day are easier to stand behind than a log reconstructed when the return is due.

For how vehicle expenses are claimed on the return, see the CRA's guide T4002, Self-employed Business, Professional, Commission, Farming, and Fishing Income.
