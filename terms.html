2. What Milepost does

Milepost lets you record trips with a date, a stated purpose, a start and destination, a distance and a category; record vehicles and odometer readings; see totals and summaries of what you have entered; and export the whole thing as a PDF or a CSV file.

3. What the numbers are, and are not

Every figure Milepost shows is arithmetic on what you typed. Distances are the ones you entered. Money figures are distance multiplied by a rate you chose. Milepost does not verify distances, does not check that a trip happened, and cannot tell whether a trip qualifies for anything.

The completeness figures — how many trips state a specific purpose, how many were written within a week, how many record both ends of the journey — are counts of your own entries against fixed, published rules described in the app. They are a prompt to write better records. They are not a legal opinion, not an audit, and not a prediction of how any authority will treat your log.

4. Record keeping stays yours

What a mileage record must contain, how long it must be kept, and what you may claim differ by country and by circumstance, and they change. Milepost cannot guarantee that a log kept in it satisfies any particular requirement. Keep your own copies: export regularly and store the files somewhere other than the phone.
